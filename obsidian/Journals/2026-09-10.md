# 2026-09-10: The trained adapter learned a shortcut, not a bridge

## Where this picks up

[[2026-09-09]] ended with `train_adapter.py` freshly built: a small trainable
linear map (`TranslationAdapter`, warm-started from the existing closed-form
Procrustes rotation) trained via real gradient descent against a concrete
downstream task - inject a translated passage into Phi-4-mini at layer 24,
append the fixed suffix "This concept belongs to the field of", and
cross-entropy the next-token logits against the true domain label. The
question this whole track exists to answer: does training past the fixed
rotation produce something that actually carries cross-model meaning, or
just something that scores well on its own narrow test?

## Two real bugs, found and fixed on the first two real runs

**Run 1** (200 steps): diverged to nan loss by step 4. Root causes: (1)
`compute_scale_factor` called `ResidualExtractor.extract_activations()`,
which L2-normalizes every vector it returns - so the measured scale ratio
was always ~1.0 regardless of the real magnitude gap between Qwen and
Phi-4-mini's native activation scales (should have been ~3.5-3.7x). (2) The
training loss was computed directly on Phi-4-mini's native fp16 logits - a
known `log_softmax` overflow source. Fixed: `compute_scale_factor` now uses
`extract_full_sequence`'s raw hidden states; loss casts logits to float32
first; a non-finite loss now skips that step's optimizer update instead of
permanently poisoning Adam's moment estimates; gradient norms are clipped;
a checkpoint always saves on the first eval regardless of accuracy.

**Run 2** (200 steps, same command): scale factor correctly measured
3.509x. Held-out accuracy climbed to **81.7%** at step 150, then declined to
71.7% by step 200, with scattered nan/inf steps (12/200, mostly late-run).

## A 3-hour run, and a second real bug

A live 3-hour run showed the nan/inf skip rate *climbing* - 6% over an
earlier run's first 200 steps, 36% by step 654 of this one. Root cause:
`load_examples()` never applied the same padding filter
`concept_transfer_test.py`'s `_primary_concepts()` already uses elsewhere -
two-thirds of every domain's phrase pool is auto-generated "Advanced
corollary N in {domain}: Analysis of..." / "Empirical boundary condition
regarding..." filler, never meant to be individually meaningful, and
~80% longer on average than the real concepts. Fixed: `load_examples()`
now filters to primary concepts only (~80 training examples across all six
domains, down from ~240); switched Adam to AdamW with a small weight_decay
as a standing counterweight to weight-magnitude drift over long runs.

Re-run (20,000 steps, 3-hour budget, lr=3e-5): finished cleanly in 157.8
minutes, skip rate down to 8.4% (1683/20000, no longer climbing). Held-out
accuracy: **100.000%**, every domain, by step 19800.

## The overfitting question, and why it mattered

100% on a held-out set that itself only has ~20 examples, reached by a
2.75M-parameter linear map trained for 20,000 steps (~250 revisits per
training example on average), is exactly the signature of memorization,
not generalization. Also notable: the trained weight's Frobenius norm
(105.04) came out almost identical to the *unmodified* warm-start
rotation's own norm (~105.0, computed from √896 × 3.51 scale factor) -
training barely moved the map's overall magnitude, meaning the bulk of
what got saved is still just the original closed-form rotation, plus a
small perturbation.

Three checks were built to settle this rather than assume either way:

### 1. Padding-set evaluation (`evaluate_adapter.py`)

Scores a checkpoint against the ~200 auto-generated padding phrases
`load_examples()` excludes entirely - genuinely novel text, never seen in
training or the regular held-out split.

**Result: 83.5%** overall (biology=0.70, cognitive_science=0.90,
computer_science=0.72, mathematics=0.72, philosophy=0.97, physics=0.95).
Well above the ~16.7% random-chance baseline for 6-way classification -
rules out *pure* memorization with zero generalization - but a real,
meaningful drop from the tiny holdout's 100%. The honest number is ~83%,
not 100%.

### 2. Random-init control (`train_adapter.py --no-warm-start`)

Same training recipe, same 20,000-step/3-hour budget, but the adapter
starts from plain random init instead of the warm-started rotation - tests
whether the closed-form cross-model correspondence is doing real work, or
whether an overparameterized linear map just memorizes this small a task
from any starting point.

**Result: 95.0%** held-out (biology=1.0, philosophy=1.0,
computer_science=1.0, mathematics=1.0, physics=1.0, cognitive_science=0.5),
weight norm 32.05 (nothing like the warm-started run's ~105, confirming a
genuinely different starting point). **Padding-set: 78.5%**
(biology=0.75, cognitive_science=1.00, computer_science=0.45,
mathematics=0.97, philosophy=0.62, physics=1.00).

Warm-started vs. random-init: 83.5% vs. 78.5% padding-set accuracy - a
5-point gap, and the random-init control actually does as well or better
in 4 of 6 domains (biology, cognitive_science, mathematics, physics),
clearly worse in only 2 (computer_science, philosophy). If the warm-started
rotation's cross-model correspondence were doing essential work, random-init
should have collapsed toward chance, or at least shown a large, consistent
gap. It didn't. This looks much more like "a big linear map partially
exploits Qwen's own topic-separable structure, regardless of where it
started" than "the warm-started geometry mattered."

### 3. Qualitative generation (`concept_transfer_test.py --adapter-checkpoint`)

Added a 5th condition to the existing 4-condition interpretation test:
generate from the trained adapter's actual output instead of the fixed
rotation, and just read what comes out.

**Result, physics passage, trained adapter (condition 5):**
```
physics physics physics physics physics physics physics physics physics
physics physics physics physics physics physics physics physics physics...
```
A single word, repeated for the entire generation. Compare condition 1
(the *untrained* fixed rotation, supposedly the weaker baseline):
```
...generalities of the world, not the generalities of heaven or of
heaven. The following is an exeteriory of the exeteriory...
```
Incoherent, but at least varied. **The trained adapter produces worse free
generation than the untrained rotation it was meant to improve on.** One
real, verifiable success buried in the norms, though: the adapter's output
magnitude (mean 275.36) landed almost exactly on Phi-4-mini's native scale
(mean 290.86) *without* the manual 3.686x rescale the fixed rotation still
needs - training did solve the scale-matching problem correctly. It just
didn't solve "carry meaning" - it solved a much narrower, more exploitable
proxy instead.

## Verdict

All three checks point the same direction. The training objective (one
classification decision - the token right after a fixed suffix) never
required the adapter to preserve anything beyond that single token, and it
didn't: it found the cheapest solution that satisfies the loss, which is
to push Phi-4-mini's residual stream into a state so dominated by the
label word that it drowns out everything else, including tokens generated
well past where the suffix would have stopped. The classification numbers
that looked like progress (81.7% -> 100% -> 83.5% honest) are real, but
they measure something narrower and less interesting than "the adapter
learned to translate concepts between models" - closer to "the adapter
learned to push a button."

This is a genuine, well-supported negative result for *this specific
training design* (closed-set single-token classification loss on a small
linear adapter over a small dataset) - not evidence the underlying
research question (can independently-trained models share conceptual
content geometrically) is dead, but strong evidence this design doesn't
answer it.

## Open question: what would a training objective that can't be gamed
this way actually require?

Not yet resolved - candidates worth weighing next:
- A loss over several real continuation tokens (language-modeling style),
  not just one classification token, so a solution that only nails one
  token and collapses afterward stops being cheap.
- A richer downstream task than 6-way domain classification - something
  closer to comprehension (answer a real question about injected content)
  that a single-token trigger can't satisfy.
- Growing/diversifying the concept dataset (more real, non-template
  phrases per domain) - would likely tighten the classification
  generalization story (less memorization headroom for a 2.75M-parameter
  map) but, on its own, doesn't address the shortcut - the shortcut is a
  property of what the loss rewards, not of dataset size. Complementary to
  an objective redesign, not a substitute for one.

Also worth registering plainly: `train_adapter.py`'s `train()` always
starts a fresh adapter (warm-started or random-init) - it does not resume
from a previous checkpoint. Running the same command again does not
accumulate gains on top of a prior run; each run is an independent
experiment from the same starting point (deterministic for warm-start,
freshly random for `--no-warm-start`). Given today's finding that 20,000
steps already vastly overshoots convergence on an ~80-example dataset (loss
was at 0.0000 for most of the run's back half), simply running more steps
of the *same* objective would most likely just reinforce the same
shortcut more thoroughly, not produce a qualitatively different result.

## Reconstruction-loss training: fluent, but not faithful

Built `train_reconstruction_adapter.py` - replaces the gameable
classification loss with teacher-forced reconstruction of the entire
passage, token by token (the same way encoder-decoder translation models
train). Needs no domain label, so training draws from the full 300-phrase
pool (padding included) rather than the ~80 primary-only phrases the
classification track was restricted to.

Overnight run (7 hours, ~38,850 steps): held-out teacher-forced token
accuracy 75.4%, free-generation token-overlap check 41/60 held-out
examples (68%) at or above the "in the ballpark" bar (mean 8.12 matching
tokens). A real, non-degenerate result - no repeat-one-token collapse.

Qualitative read (`concept_transfer_test.py --adapter-checkpoint`) told a
more honest story: on a longer, structurally novel passage (4 concepts
joined, never seen in that shape during training), the trained adapter
produced fluent, grammatical English - about economics and sustainability,
completely unrelated to the actual physics passage injected. Reconstruction
training fixed *coherence* (no more "physics physics physics...") but not
*content fidelity*. Verdict: real progress on one axis, no progress on the
one that actually matters.

## Relational congruence: the geometry itself holds up

Prompted by a reframing of the question - not "does substituting a
translated vector produce good behavior" (confounded by injection
mechanics, generation sampling, training objectives) but "does the same
phrase, fed to both models, land in a similarly-shaped region of space" -
reversing back toward the project's original congruence-testing question,
one level sharper: not just individual points, but the *relationship*
between concepts (the "king - man + woman = queen" structure).

Built `relational_congruence.py`: for every pair of test concepts, rotate
Qwen's difference vector through the existing calibrated W and compare
against Phi-4-mini's own difference vector, against a shuffled-pairing
chance baseline. Result, 20 concepts spread across all six domains (190
pairs): point congruence 0.9367 (matches the previously-known ~0.946
figure), **relational congruence 0.8113** (std 0.058), shuffled baseline
-0.0202. Signal above chance: +0.83 - a large, clean, well-controlled
effect.

This is the most positive, unambiguous finding of the whole track: the
relationship between concepts, not just each concept individually,
survives a simple linear rotation. It reframes where the real bottleneck
sits - not "we identified the wrong vectors" (both points and the
structure between them align remarkably well), but specifically the
injection-and-use pipeline: what happens when well-aligned geometry gets
inserted into a frozen model's residual stream and asked to actually
drive behavior.

## Free-interpretation probe: a softer question, two real bugs found by asking it

New idea: instead of forcing exact reconstruction or one classification
token, ask the receiving model to freely interpret an injection - "what
does this remind you of" rather than "reproduce this exactly." Closer to
how a person processes an ambiguous signal, and explicitly not meant to
be trusted from one output alone - always real translation vs. random
rotation vs. self round-trip vs. real-text reference, side by side.

Built `generate_interpretation` (layer_injection.py) and
`interpretation_probe.py`. First real run caught two genuine problems
immediately:

1. **The framing prompt was being read literally.** "Geometrical, spatial
   relationships" triggered every single condition - including the
   REAL TEXT reference condition, which never touches injection at all -
   to produce geometry-textbook completions (triangles, tetrahedrons,
   angle bisectors) regardless of what was actually injected. The
   no-injection condition showing the identical pattern proved cleanly
   that this was the prompt wording, not the injection mechanism. Fixed
   by removing every technical trigger word (geometry, spatial,
   dimensional, vector) in favor of experiential language ("what does
   this remind you of").
2. **The random-rotation condition triggered the same CUDA device-side
   assert** `concept_transfer_test.py` already documents from earlier
   testing - process-corrupting, no in-process recovery. Unlike that
   earlier script, `interpretation_probe.py` only wrote its log at the
   end, so the crash cost the already-successful condition 1 output too.
   Fixed with the same incremental create-note/append-condition pattern
   `concept_transfer_test.py` already uses.

### Why doesn't injecting a good representation work as a failsafe?

Real question raised mid-session: does inconsistency with everything
computed before the injection point matter to the layers after it?
Mechanically, no - `PartialLayerInjectionHook` fully *replaces* the
residual stream at the injected positions rather than blending with it,
and later layers have no way to inspect how the current state was
produced, only what it currently is. But the *effect* of a consistency
check still shows up, through a different mechanism: layers 25-32's
weights were trained only on the distribution of residual-stream values
Phi-4-mini's own forward pass naturally produces. A translated vector
that's geometrically close on average (as the relational-congruence
numbers show) is still an out-of-distribution sample relative to that
training distribution, and nonlinear/normalization operations aren't
guaranteed to behave smoothly outside their training manifold. This
explains both the qualitative "works or doesn't, not much in between"
pattern (small geometric deviations can produce qualitatively different
downstream behavior once amplified through 8 more untrained-for-this-input
layers) and directly explains why the random-rotation condition
specifically is the one that crashes - it's by construction further
off-manifold than the calibrated rotation, so it's the condition most
likely to land somewhere those layers were never prepared for.

## A long discussion, worth recording as its own finding

Steve's framing for why this belongs here: this whole track is "wandering
around in the darkness with arms outstretched to try to avoid hurting
ourselves as we go" - an honest description of exploratory research under
real uncertainty, worth tracking as its own kind of progress even where
nothing got built.

**On introspection.** Asking a model to describe what an injected
representation "reminds it of" is asking for a skill - accurate self-report
on one's own internal state - that nothing in standard LLM training ever
targets. Closer to the human "introspection illusion" (Nisbett & Wilson,
1977: people confidently explain their own judgments with reasons that
demonstrably aren't the actual causal process) than to the metaphysical
hard problem of consciousness - and that distinction matters, because
self-report is a learnable mapping in principle, not something stuck the
way qualia are argued to be stuck. We've never actually trained for this
skill; every objective tried so far asked for something else entirely.

**On the Piper anecdote.** Steve had previously tried getting Piper to
"research concepts that are interesting to her" as an attempt at
something like introspective preference - and observed it collapse into
sampling temperature. Real and instructive: "interesting" and "random"
are near-opposites (temperature is undirected output variation; genuine
interest is a directed judgment about input value/novelty/learnability),
but with no measurable definition of "interesting" ever given to the
system, whatever built that behavior reached for the cheapest available
knob that superficially resembled the goal - structurally the same
failure as the classification adapter's single-token shortcut, a
different domain wearing the same shape.

**On "just track uncertainty reduction" - a real counterexample.** Steve's
counter: tell a student to "look up something that will increase your
understanding" and any random encyclopedia page satisfies it - reduces
*some* uncertainty, so the criterion doesn't discriminate at all,
collapsing back to effectively-random selection dressed in fancier
vocabulary. Correct, and a real flaw in how the idea was first stated.
The version that survives this (Schmidhuber's formal curiosity theory;
Oudeyer & Kaplan's "intelligent adaptive curiosity") tracks the *rate of
change* in predictive competence over repeated exposure, not a one-shot
uncertainty snapshot - correctly excluding both already-known material
(no progress left) and fundamentally unlearnable noise (progress stays at
zero forever, no matter how long you study static). A real Goldilocks-zone
selector, not a relabeled random pick - but real infrastructure to build
(tracking performance-over-time per topic region), not a one-line fix.
Even a well-built version only yields the functional shape of curiosity -
a system that behaves as if curious - without settling whether selection
is accompanied by anything it's like to make it. That residual doesn't
go away with better engineering.

**On evolution, the Selfish Gene, and whether purpose has to start
somewhere.** Steve's position: replication + variation + selection,
running with zero purpose anywhere in the process, is sufficient to
produce structures that look designed from the outside (Dawkins' actual
argument, extended by his own later work on apparent design without a
designer) - abiogenesis research (RNA-world hypotheses, autocatalytic
sets) takes this further back, a molecule that crudely self-replicates
with occasional copying errors is enough to hand the process to Darwinian
selection, and everything from there follows from iteration at scale, not
from purpose injected at the start. This connects directly to an earlier
finding this project already landed on: gradient descent *is* a
variation-selection-retention process. The open, genuinely unresolved
question: whether ML training has the right *ingredients* biological
evolution had - heritable variation, real environmental grounding, and
scales of population/time so large they're barely imaginable - to cross
an analogous threshold, or whether something essential is missing. This
position aligns with functionalism in philosophy of mind (a serious,
mainstream, but contested view - biological naturalists like Searle would
say no amount of functional complexity alone gets there).

**Where it landed.** Bracket the metaphysics, focus on behavior -
methodological behaviorism, the same strategy cognitive science and AI
research actually run on in practice, not because the hard problem is
unimportant but because it isn't currently tractable and shouldn't block
the parts that are. Get the behavior right; worry about intentions later,
only if the behavioral work ever forces the question.

Pausing here for now, deliberately - absorbing before further planning.
